When should I hire a general contractor instead of individual subcontractors?

You need a general contractor when a project involves multiple trades, structural alterations, or required building permits. Simple single-trade tasks, like replacing a standard water heater or painting a room, can usually be handled directly by specialized subcontractors or handy services. Hiring a general contractor removes the burden of managing conflicting subcontractor schedules and resolves quality issues through a single point of accountability.

What is the difference between a general contractor and a subcontractor?

A general contractor oversees entire construction projects from start to finish, while a subcontractor specializes in a single trade. Subcontractors—such as framing crews, plumbers, roofers, and electricians—are hired directly by the general contractor to execute specific parts of the scope. Choosing to act as your own general contractor trade-off requires you to take on full liability for worker coordination, site safety, and scheduling delays. Working with a dedicated general contractor provides a single accountable party who verifies each subcontractor's licensing, insurance, and work standard.

An inspector examines surface temperatures, relative humidity levels, insulation continuity, and ventilation pathways. Unlike a general home inspection, this assessment focuses directly on building envelope physics to separate plumbing failures from warm, moist air contacting cold surfaces. For property owners, understanding this difference determines whether you need targeted insulation upgrades, mechanical ventilation improvements, or structural air sealing to protect structural framing and drywall.

What is the difference between NRC and CAC ratings for ceiling tiles?

NRC measures sound absorption within a single room, while CAC measures sound transmission blocking between adjacent rooms. Choosing the right tile depends on your specific noise problem.

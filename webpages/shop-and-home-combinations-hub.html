Shop-and-Home Combinations

✓ Schema.org Validated Last updated: 2026-10-21

Type: WebPage

Combining your workspace and your residence into a single steel structure offers unmatched convenience and efficiency. We specialize in the design and construction of shop-and-home combinations that blend residential comfort with heavy-duty metal building durability.

How Total Building Systems Inc. can help

We help you design and build integrated shop-and-home combinations that maximize your property's utility. By using pre-engineered metal building systems, we create a seamless structure that houses both your high-clearance workshop or garage and your primary living quarters under one durable roof.

Integrated Living and Workspace Design

A shop-and-home combination (often called a 'shouse') is an efficient way to gain large-scale storage or workspace without the cost of two separate buildings. This configuration is ideal for those who need an RV garage, a commercial-grade workshop, or hobby space directly accessible from their home. We provide the structural steel expertise to ensure the tall spans required for the shop integrate safely with the residential framing. Our team handles the design-to-completion process, ensuring the transition between the work and living areas is functional and code-compliant.

Environmental and Sound Separation

One of the most critical parts of a combined-use project is managing the environment between the shop and the home. We perform condensation assessments and install specialized vapor-control insulation to keep the shop area comfortable. For the home side, we use sound-control improvements and interior partitions to ensure that workshop noise doesn't disrupt the residential space. Our selective interior demolition and framing capabilities allow us to customize the layout to your specific workflow.

Questions to consider before you decide

  • Fire-rated wall requirements between the living space and the workshop.
  • Ventilation and odor control to prevent shop fumes from entering the home.
  • Zoning and permit restrictions for combined-use residential structures.
  • Separate HVAC zoning for the workshop and living quarters.
